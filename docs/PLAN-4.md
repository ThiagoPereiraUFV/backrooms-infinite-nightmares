# Backrooms - Infinite Nightmares — Phase 4 Plan: Lore-Faithful Level Bodies

Continuation of [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) (MVP, M0–M7), [PLAN-2.md](PLAN-2.md) (M8–M13, shipped) and
[PLAN-3.md](PLAN-3.md) (M14–M17, shipped).

PLAN-3 ended with an explicit promise: _"This plan adds no new map detail to the nine levels. It
clears the space for that work; the actual deepening (bespoke motifs, per-level props, set pieces)
is a follow-up phase."_ **This is that phase.** The roster is fixed, the profile type is complete
and compiler-enforced, and every generation/render/audio consumer already reads from it. What is
missing is that all nine levels are still the _same building_ wearing nine palettes.

## 1. Problem statement

Every level today is generated by the same pipeline with nine sets of numbers:

- **One surface treatment for all nine.** [proceduralTextures.ts](../src/components/scene/proceduralTextures.ts)
  paints vertical wallpaper striping on every wall, mottled carpet on every floor and a 2×2 drop-tile
  grid on every ceiling — including the cave and the flooded level. Only the palette differs.
- **One lighting behavior for all nine.** [GameScene.tsx](../src/components/scene/GameScene.tsx)
  `LevelLighting` dims one global `AmbientLight` at random. "Flickering fluorescents" is currently
  the whole world blinking at once, and the emissive fixture planes never change.
  `lightSpacing` is honored even on levels that canonically have _no light sources at all_.
- **No doors, no openings, no breaches.** [chunk.ts](../src/engine/generation/chunk.ts) knows three
  cell values (`CELL_OPEN`, `CELL_WALL`, `CELL_PILLAR`). `fillRooms` carves "doors" by setting a cell
  open — there is no door object, no frame, nothing to see. Level 5's defining physical trait (a
  hotel is _made of doors_) is invisible.
- **One enemy for all nine.** `entityRegistry` has exactly one entry, `wanderer`
  ([wanderer.ts](../src/engine/entities/wanderer.ts)), rendered as a 0.5 × 1.8 × 0.3 box with red
  emissive. All nine levels list it in their `spawnTable`.
- **Four synthesized ambience recipes for nine levels**, and no sound assets at all: `public/` contains
  exactly one file, `.nojekyll`.

The goal of this phase: **each of the Main Nine reads as its own place** — its own surfaces, ceiling
behavior, lighting behavior, structural features, props, inhabitants and soundscape — matching the
Backrooms wiki as closely as this engine practically allows, without touching the determinism,
border-contract or connectivity guarantees that make the world work.

**Recommendation: keep `backrooms.fandom.com` as the single source of truth**, i.e. the roster
already shipped. Reasons, in order of weight:

1. PLAN-3 §2.2 records that this category page was **fetched live** and states its own definition of
   the Main Nine as levels 0–8. It is not a guess.
2. The names are already load-bearing: `levelProfile.ts` literals, the HUD badge, the enter overlay
   (`Level 0 — The Lobby`), `levelProfile.test.ts` identity assertions, and two `e2e/game-flow.spec.ts`
   assertions (`"Lights Out"`, `Level 0 — The Lobby`). Renaming four levels is a content regression
   dressed as research (the exact argument PLAN-3 Design Note D2 made about renumbering).
3. Mixing sources per level is the worst option: it produces a roster that matches no wiki.

**The implementer must not decide this silently.** Confirm with the user first. If the user picks
`backrooms-wiki.fandom.com` instead, that is a legitimate choice but it adds a rename/retheme
milestone (levels 1, 4, 6, 7 change identity, and Level 7 flips from water to suburbs, invalidating
§5.7 and the `FLOODED_DEPTHS` palette) — plan for it explicitly rather than absorbing it.

### 2.1 Mandatory per-level verification (do not author from memory)

Every physical value in §5 below is **provisional** — written from general knowledge, unverified.
Before the M24 authoring pass, the implementer must fetch each level's page with `WebFetch`
(fall back to `WebSearch` if the fetch is blocked) and extract:

- **Wall / floor / ceiling material and color** as stated in prose ("mono-yellow wallpaper",
  "damp carpet", "poured concrete", "riveted steel").
- **Any stated dimension** — ceiling height, corridor width, room scale. Convert to meters; if the
  wiki states nothing, keep the shipped value and say so in a comment.
- **Doors / doorways / passages** — does the level have them, how many, are they open or closed.
- **Holes / breaches / openings** in walls, floors or ceilings.
- **Lighting** — fixture type, whether lights work, flicker, emergency lighting, total darkness.
- **Atmosphere** — humidity, temperature, standing water, smell/sound cues, fog/haze.
- **Entities** listed as inhabiting the level, and whether they are hostile or passive.
- **Hazards** (steam, electricity, water) — these mostly become non-goals (§3), but record them.

Record, at the top of [levelProfile.ts](../src/engine/generation/levelProfile.ts), next to the
existing roster table: the wiki domain chosen in D0, the per-level page URLs, and the retrieval date.
That doc comment is the audit trail for why the numbers are what they are; the next reader should
never have to guess whether a value is lore or vibes.

Where the wiki is silent or self-contradictory, **keep the shipped value and add a one-line comment
saying the wiki does not specify it.** Inventing detail and presenting it as lore is worse than
admitting the gap.

## 3. Scope

### In scope

1. **Per-level surface identity** — a `surfaceStyle` selecting a distinct procedural wall/floor/ceiling
   painter per level, plus a real `footstepSurface`.
2. **Structural features** — door frames, wall breaches, ceiling openings and ceiling pipe/duct runs,
   derived deterministically from the finished cell grid.
3. **Per-level lighting behavior** — fixture kind, whether fixtures exist at all, and a pure,
   testable flicker model including per-fixture flicker.
4. **Level props** — pipes, lockers, barrels, transformers, panels, rubble, delivered as additions to
   the existing furniture catalog (ground props) and the feature system (overhead props).
5. **Entities per level** — a small set of lore-appropriate entities behind two or three shared
   behavior strategies, each with its own silhouette and audio cue, wired through the existing
   `spawnTable` seam.
6. **Sound assets** — a `SampledAudioEngine` that plays downloaded CC0 loops/one-shots and falls back
   to the shipped procedural synthesis for anything missing, with nine per-level ambience ids.
7. **Docs** — CLAUDE.md, README, and the source-of-truth comment block in `levelProfile.ts`.

### Explicit non-goals (do not build these)

- **Vertical movement / falling.** Collision is 2D (XZ) and the camera is pinned at
  `PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT`. "Holes in the floor" therefore become _visual recesses that block movement_,
  not pits you fall into (§6.3). A fall system is a phase of its own.
- **Damage hazards** (Level 2 scalding steam, Level 3 live electricity, drowning). `stats.ts` can
  take damage, but a hazard-volume system, its telegraphing and its balancing are new gameplay, not
  set dressing. Steam and sparks are rendered/heard, never harmful, this phase.
- **Real per-fixture point lights.** three.js pays per light per material; 25 chunks × ~30 fixtures
  is not affordable. Fixtures glow (emissive) and modulate the ambient — that is the whole lighting
  model (§7).
- **A physics engine, destructible geometry, openable doors.** Door frames are scenery with no
  collider (§6.2 explains why this is a correctness requirement, not laziness).
- **Downloaded 3D models** — deferred with a costed checklist in §11, not silently omitted.
- **Downloaded wall/floor/ceiling textures.** Procedural canvas textures cost zero bytes, zero
  licensing and already parameterize on palette + decay (§Design Note D3).
- **New level-select UI, progression, save games, multiplayer.** Unchanged from PLAN-3 §3.
- **Changes to `edgeGateways`, `ensureConnectivity`, the LRU cache, collision resolution, or the
  fixed-timestep loop.** Every new system is additive and reads the grid after it is final.

## 4. Data model — extending `LevelProfile`

### 4.1 Rules for the extension

- **Every new field must have a consumer in this phase.** No "for later" fields — PLAN-3 Design Note
  D4 made completeness a compile-time obligation, so a speculative field is a permanent tax on every
  future level literal.
- **Flat scalar per renderer knob; a nested record only when the values always travel together.**
  `surfaceStyle`, `lighting` and `footstepSurface` each feed exactly one consumer, so they stay flat
  like the existing sixteen. `featureRates` is nested because all of its members are consumed by one
  generation pass as a unit.
- **No `Partial`, no optionals.** Adding a required field makes TypeScript walk the implementer
  through all nine literals — that is the point.
- **Do not restructure the existing sixteen fields.** `wallDensity`, `ceilingHeight`, `fogDensity`,
  `lightIntensity`, `flickerAmount`, `decay`, `lightSpacing` and the rest keep their names and
  consumers. Renaming them would churn nine files for zero behavior.
- **Humidity gets no field.** It is expressible entirely through fields that already have consumers:
  `footstepSurface: "wet"`, a damp `surfaceStyle` painter, a drip-heavy ambience id, and
  `fogDensity`. Adding a `humidity: number` nobody reads would be exactly the speculative field the
  first rule forbids.

### 4.2 New types (in `levelProfile.ts`, beside `GeometryStyle`/`AmbienceId`)

```ts
/** Which procedural painter set dresses this level's wall/floor/ceiling. */
export type SurfaceStyle =
  | "dampWallpaper" // L0 — mono-yellow paper, moist carpet, drop tiles
  | "rawConcrete" // L1 — poured slab, form-tie marks, no ceiling tiles
  | "rivetedSteel" // L2 — painted steel plate, plate floor, pipe-crowded ceiling
  | "rustedUtility" // L3 — block wall, sealed concrete, cable tray ceiling
  | "officeDrywall" // L4 — bleached drywall, low-pile carpet, drop tiles
  | "hotelPaper" // L5 — patterned paper + wainscot, figured carpet
  | "voidBlack" // L6 — near-black everything, no legible detail
  | "wetTile" // L7 — glazed tile with grout, standing-water sheen
  | "bareRock"; // L8 — mottled stone, no tiles, no skirting

/** What kind of fixture this level's ceiling lights are, and how they behave. */
export type LightingMode =
  | "fluorescentPanels" // recessed troffers: stutter flicker, audible buzz
  | "cagedIndustrial" // sparse caged bulbs / strip lights: slow brownout sag
  | "emergencyOnly" // few amber/red fixtures: steady slow pulse
  | "none"; // no fixtures generated at all — player lamp/flashlight only

/** Surface the player's feet land on — drives footstep timbre. */
export type FootstepSurface = "carpet" | "hard" | "wet" | "gravel";

/**
 * How often each cosmetic structural feature appears, as a 0..1 chance per
 * eligible cell. All three are consumed by one pass (placeFeatures), so they
 * live together.
 */
export interface LevelFeatureRates {
  /** Door frame on a detected threshold cell (open cell pinched by walls). */
  doorway: number;
  /** Broken-drywall / crumbled hole on a wall cell that faces open space. */
  wallBreach: number;
  /** Missing ceiling tile / opening above an open cell. */
  ceilingOpening: number;
  /** Pipe or duct run along the ceiling of an open cell. */
  ceilingRun: number;
}
```

`LevelProfile` gains exactly four fields:

```ts
export interface LevelProfile {
  // ... the existing sixteen, unchanged ...
  surfaceStyle: SurfaceStyle;
  lighting: LightingMode;
  footstepSurface: FootstepSurface;
  featureRates: LevelFeatureRates;
}
```

`AmbienceId` widens from four shared recipes to **nine per-level ids** so each level can key its own
audio file (§10.3):

```ts
export type AmbienceId =
  | "lobbyHum"
  | "parkingDrone"
  | "pipeSteam"
  | "stationBuzz"
  | "officeSilence"
  | "hotelWind"
  | "blackSilence"
  | "floodedDeep"
  | "caveDrip";
```

## 6. M19 — Structural features, and how they stay clear of the border contract

### 6.1 Where the pass runs, and why that is the whole safety argument

New pure module `src/engine/generation/placeFeatures.ts`, called from `generateChunk` **after**
`ensureConnectivity` and alongside the existing furniture/spawn passes:

```ts
const features = placeFeatures({
  cells, // read-only — this pass never writes
  anchors, // the same gateway + center anchors furniture and spawns already respect
  rng: createRng(hashInts(worldSeed, cx, cz, 0xd006)),
  rates: profile.featureRates,
});
```

Four properties make this structurally incapable of breaking the border contract or connectivity:

1. **It reads the grid; it never writes it.** `cells` is passed as `Readonly<Uint8Array>` in the
   argument type and the pass returns a new array. The test in §12.2 asserts the grid is byte-identical
   before and after — the guarantee is checked, not just intended.
2. **It runs last.** Gateways are already forced open and `ensureConnectivity` has already carved
   its paths, so nothing this pass observes can be invalidated later.
3. **It never emits colliders.** Door frames, breaches, ceiling openings and pipe runs contribute
   nothing to `ChunkManager.obstaclesIn`. A cosmetic object that cannot block a cell cannot pinch off
   a guaranteed path (§6.2).
4. **`edgeGateways` is not touched.** It hashes `(worldSeed, orientation, edgeA, edgeB)` and has
   never seen a `LevelProfile` — PLAN-3 §4.3 already established this immunity, and it still holds.

Anchor cells are excluded anyway (cheap belt-and-braces, and it keeps frames out of the exact cells
the connectivity carver relies on).

The salt `0xd006` follows the established convention (`0xfa57` furniture, `0x57a4` spawns): one
independent stream per pass, so a feature-rate change never reshuffles furniture or item placement.

### 6.2 Why doors have no collider (a correctness requirement, not a shortcut)

The connectivity guarantee is expressed **entirely in cell values**: `ensureConnectivity` flood-fills
over `cells[idx] === CELL_OPEN`, and `chunk.test.ts` asserts every gateway is reachable from the chunk
center through open cells. Collision, however, is resolved against `ObstacleWorld` AABBs, which is a
_different_ representation. A closed door would be an AABB filling an open cell — invisible to the
connectivity proof, and capable of sealing the single carved corridor to a gateway. That is precisely
the class of bug PLAN-2 M8 was written to eliminate ("render and collision must never disagree").

So: **door frames are scenery.** A frame is drawn around the threshold; where a leaf is drawn, it is
drawn swung open against the wall. If a level ever needs a genuinely closed door, the correct
representation is a `CELL_WALL` — which the generator already produces — not a collider bolted onto
an open cell.

### 6.3 Feature kinds, detection, and rendering

```ts
export type FeatureKind = "doorway" | "wallBreach" | "ceilingOpening" | "ceilingRun";

export interface ChunkFeature {
  kind: FeatureKind;
  cellX: number;
  cellZ: number;
  /** 0 = the feature spans the X axis, 1 = the Z axis. */
  axis: 0 | 1;
}
// ChunkData gains: features: ChunkFeature[];
```

| Kind             | Eligibility (pure, from the final grid)                                                                              | Rendered as                                                                                                 |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `doorway`        | open cell whose two neighbors on one axis are solid and whose two neighbors on the other axis are open — a threshold | merged-box frame (two jambs + head) inset to the cell boundary, plus an open leaf at ~100° on the jamb      |
| `wallBreach`     | a `CELL_WALL` cell with at least one open neighbor                                                                   | a dark recessed panel + broken-edge boxes on the wall face toward the open side                             |
| `ceilingOpening` | an open cell that carries no light fixture                                                                           | a dark quad 4 cm below the ceiling plane, with a thin frame — reads as a missing tile from below (see note) |
| `ceilingRun`     | an open cell with an open neighbor on the run axis                                                                   | 2–3 long cylinders/boxes hung just under the ceiling, spanning the cell on `axis`                           |

**Note on ceiling openings.** The ceiling is one plane per chunk, so a real hole would require cutting
geometry. Because the player is always below it and the level is fogged, a dark quad placed slightly
below the plane is visually indistinguishable from a hole and costs one instance. This is a
deliberate illusion — worth a one-line comment in the renderer, because it is exactly the kind of
non-obvious choice a future reader would otherwise "fix".

**Floor holes are not implemented.** With XZ-only collision and a fixed eye height there is no
correct behavior for walking into one. If the lore for a level demands them, the honest cheap version
is a recessed dark box that _is_ a collider (walk around it), which means it must be placed by the
furniture pass (which already proves corridors survive), not by this pass. Recommend cutting them.

### 6.4 Rendering integration

- New `src/components/scene/featureGeometry.ts`, an exact structural twin of
  [furnitureGeometry.ts](../src/components/scene/furnitureGeometry.ts): module-level `Map` cache,
  merged unit-box builders via `mergeBufferGeometries`, a dev-only completeness check that every
  `FeatureKind` has a builder, never disposed. **Reuse that file's shape rather than inventing a
  second convention** — a reader who understands one understands both.
- [ChunkMesh.tsx](../src/components/scene/ChunkMesh.tsx) `buildPlacements` returns one more
  `Map<FeatureKind, InstancePlacement>` and renders it through the existing `Instances` component
  (`dispose={null}`, matrices set in `useLayoutEffect`). No new rendering machinery.
- [levelMaterials.ts](../src/components/scene/levelMaterials.ts) `LevelMaterials` gains `doorFrame`,
  `breach` and `voidDark`, derived from the palette exactly like `furnitureWood`/`furnitureFabric`
  are today. They are created and disposed inside the existing `useMemo`/`useEffect` pair, so the
  play→menu→play leak test covers them for free — **do not create materials anywhere else.**

## 7. M20 — Lighting behavior

### 7.1 `chunk.ts`: `lighting: "none"` means no fixtures

The light-placement loop becomes conditional on the mode. Everything else about it is unchanged.
This is what finally makes Level 6 (and possibly Level 8) dark, because today's fixtures are
`MeshBasicMaterial` and therefore glow at full brightness no matter how low `lightIntensity` is.

### 7.2 A pure flicker module (the testability seam)

Today's flicker lives inside `LevelLighting`'s `useFrame` and calls `Math.random()` — untestable,
and it makes the entire world blink in unison. Move the math out:

```ts
// src/engine/lighting/flicker.ts — pure, no three.js, no DOM
/** Multiplier in [0, 1] applied to a fixture's or the ambient's base intensity. */
export function flickerFactor(
  mode: LightingMode,
  amount: number,
  timeSeconds: number,
  seed: number,
): number;
```

Per-mode curves: `fluorescentPanels` = mostly 1 with short stutters and rare long dropouts;
`cagedIndustrial` = slow sag/brownout; `emergencyOnly` = steady slow pulse; `none` = constant 1
(nothing is on anyway). Determinism comes from hashing `(seed, floor(timeSeconds * rate))` through
the existing `hashInts` — no `Math.random`, no accumulated state, trivially unit-testable, and
identical on every machine.

`LevelLighting` becomes a thin consumer: `ambient.intensity = base * flickerFactor(profile.lighting,
profile.flickerAmount, clock.elapsedTime, worldSeed)`.

### 7.3 Per-fixture flicker (the biggest atmosphere win)

Give the light-fixture `InstancedMesh` an `instanceColor` attribute and rewrite it at ~10 Hz (never
per frame — same discipline as the HUD's 10 Hz snapshots), seeding `flickerFactor` with the fixture's
cell index so each panel blinks on its own schedule. Cost: one small `Float32Array` upload per chunk
per 100 ms across ≤ 25 chunks. Enable it for `fluorescentPanels` and `cagedIndustrial`; skip it for
`emergencyOnly` (steady) and `none` (no fixtures).

**Risk to check first:** confirm in three 0.185 that `InstancedMesh.instanceColor` multiplies with a
`MeshBasicMaterial`'s color. If it does not, the fallback is a per-chunk uniform (all fixtures in a
chunk share a phase) — still far better than today's global blink, and it needs no attribute plumbing.
Decide this with a two-minute spike before building the 10 Hz updater.

### 7.4 Fixture shape per mode

A small lookup table beside the renderer — `Record<LightingMode, { geometry, offsetY, rotX }>` —
selects a recessed troffer plane, a small caged box, or an amber dome. This is a table, not a class
hierarchy: four entries, one consumer, no behavior. A strategy _object_ here would be pattern
theater (KISS).

A `sconces` mode (wall-mounted hotel lights) was considered and **cut**: it needs wall-adjacency data
the light pass does not have, and at this fog density a warm-tinted ceiling fixture is
indistinguishable. Not worth a new data dependency.

## 8. M21 — Props: extend furniture, do not build a parallel system

Ground props (`barrel`, `lockers`, `pipeStack`, `valveWheel`, `transformer`, `electricalPanel`,
`vendingMachine`, `waterCooler`, `luggageCart`, `rubblePile`, `stalagmite`) go into the existing
[catalog.ts](../src/engine/furniture/catalog.ts) as new `FurnitureDef` entries plus builders in
[furnitureGeometry.ts](../src/components/scene/furnitureGeometry.ts). That system already provides
everything a prop system would need: per-level weights, deterministic placement, fit + corridor
tests, the sub-cell connectivity safety net, exact colliders and instanced rendering. Building
`engine/props/` next to `engine/furniture/` would be duplicated machinery for identical requirements.

**Adding catalog entries is provably non-breaking for existing levels.** `pickDef` filters out
entries whose weight is `<= 0` _before_ drawing, and draws exactly one `rng.next()` regardless of
catalog size — so a level that does not list a new prop produces a byte-identical `furniture` array.
Verify this holds with a determinism test pinned against pre-change fixtures for one level.

Two small extensions are needed:

- `FurnitureMaterialRole` widens from `"wood" | "fabric"` to include `"metal"` and `"stone"`, with two
  matching shared materials in `useLevelMaterials` (derived from the palette, decay-darkened, exactly
  like the existing two).
- Overhead props (pipes, ducts, cable trays) are **not** furniture — they are above head height, need
  no collider and no corridor test. They are the `ceilingRun` feature from §6.3. Two systems total,
  split on a real distinction (collides vs. does not), not three.

## 9. M22 — Entities per level

### 9.1 What exists

One definition (`wanderer`), one behavior function, one box mesh, rendered by an `InstancedMesh`
that [PlayerRig.tsx](../src/components/scene/PlayerRig.tsx) owns and updates inside its simulation
`useFrame`. `activeEntitySpawns` thins by `difficulty.enemyAggression`, which is what makes
peaceful spawn nothing as a pure-function invariant.

### 9.2 Behavior strategies (a real Strategy, because there are now several)

Going from one entity to five by copying `updateWanderer` five times would duplicate roam, aggro,
chase and contact-damage logic that differs only in numbers. Extract
`src/engine/entities/behaviors.ts` with **at most three** factories:

- `createChaser(params)` — today's wanderer logic, parameterized: `roamSpeed`, `chaseSpeedBase/Max`,
  `aggroRadius`, `deaggroRadius`, `contactRadius`, `damagePerSecond`, `radius`.
- `createStalker(params)` — advances only while the player is not facing it, freezes when observed.
- `createDrifter(params)` — wanders, never chases, deals no damage (ambient dread).

Each entity then becomes a **data entry** in `src/engine/entities/catalog.ts`: id, display name,
behavior + params, appearance id, audio cue id. Adding a lore entity stays "a registry entry, not a
refactor", exactly as CLAUDE.md promises. Which levels an entity inhabits is already expressed by
`LevelProfile.spawnTable` weights — no new mechanism.

Two contract changes fall out, both small and worth doing:

- `EntityContext` gains `playerForward: { x: number; z: number }` — `createStalker` cannot exist
  without it, and `PlayerRig` already has the yaw.
- `EntityDefinition.spawn(x, z)` gains an `rng: Rng` parameter. Today `updateWanderer` picks roam
  targets with `Math.random()`, so entity motion is not reproducible and behavior tests would have to
  mock a global. Seeding from the spawn key makes every behavior deterministic and unit-testable with
  no mocking — the cheapest possible testability seam, taken while the file is open anyway.

### 9.3 Rendering: move it out of `PlayerRig`

`PlayerRig` currently runs the simulation **and** owns the enemy `InstancedMesh` — two unrelated
jobs, and the second one blocks having more than one silhouette. Extract
`src/components/scene/EntitiesField.tsx`, which takes the `EntitySystem` instance as a prop and does
its own `useFrame` transform write: one `InstancedMesh` per registered definition, geometry from a
new `entityGeometry(defId)` built on the same cached-merged-box pattern as furniture and features.

Keep the two hard-won details from the current code: `frustumCulled={false}` (the bounding sphere is
cached from the first render at count 0 and would cull the mesh forever) and `dispose={null}` on the
shared geometry/material.

**Do not add per-level appearance overrides for an entity.** A level's flavor comes from _which_
entities its spawn table lists; per-level reskins would multiply meshes and materials for a
difference nobody sees through fog (YAGNI).

### 9.4 Audio cues

`AudioEngine.playGrowl()` → `playEntityCue(cue: EntityCueId)` with a small union
(`"growl" | "shriek" | "chitter" | "laugh"`). One parameterized method rather than four methods keeps
the interface narrow (ISP) and keeps `NullAudioEngine` a one-liner. Call sites: `PlayerRig`'s growl
block only. The growl re-arm logic (`growledKeys`) is unchanged; it just reads the cue id off the
entity definition.

Entity naming is the part of this plan most likely to be wrong from memory. Fetch each level page's

## 10. M23 — Sound assets

### 10.1 Engine shape: sampled, with the procedural engine as the floor

The `AudioEngine` interface was designed for exactly this swap. Add
`src/engine/audio/SampledAudioEngine.ts`:

```ts
export class SampledAudioEngine implements AudioEngine {
  constructor(
    private readonly ctx: AudioContext,
    private readonly manifest: AudioManifest,
    /** Every cue with no decoded buffer delegates here. */
    private readonly fallback: AudioEngine,
    private readonly loader: AudioAssetLoader = fetchAudioAsset,
  ) {}
}
```

The fallback is not defensive padding — it is the design:

- The manifest can be **partial**. Assets land level by level, cue by cue, and the game always has a
  full soundscape because the shipped `ProceduralAudioEngine` covers anything missing.
- **Network and decode failures degrade to sound, not silence.** A 404 under the GitHub Pages base
  path, or Vorbis on an old Safari, produces the MVP soundscape instead of a dead game.
- Both the loader and the `AudioContext` are constructor-injected, so the whole class is testable
  against the fake context harness already written in
  [ProceduralAudioEngine.test.ts](../src/engine/audio/ProceduralAudioEngine.test.ts) — no network, no
  real Web Audio.

Loading is asynchronous and must never block entering play: `startAmbience(id)` immediately starts the
fallback's synthesis, kicks off the fetch/decode for that level's loop, and crossfades to the sampled
loop when it resolves (a short crossfade also hides any encoder padding at the loop point). Only the
**current level's** ambience is fetched. One-shots (footsteps, cues, UI) are small enough to fetch on
first use.

`GameRoot` composes them where it already constructs the engine after the user gesture:
`new SampledAudioEngine(ctx, MANIFEST, new ProceduralAudioEngine(ctx))`. Both must share one
`AudioContext` — note that `ProceduralAudioEngine`'s constructor already accepts an injected context,
and its `dispose()` closes it, so `SampledAudioEngine.dispose()` must delegate rather than close it
twice.

### 10.2 Asset paths and the base-path trap

Next.js does **not** rewrite URLs inside `fetch()`. `fetch("/audio/ambience/lobbyHum.ogg")` works on
`localhost` and 404s on GitHub Pages, where everything lives under
`/backrooms-infinite-nightmares/`. Add one helper and route every asset URL through it:

```ts
// src/config/assets.ts
const BASE_PATH = process.env.NEXT_PUBLIC_BASE_PATH ?? "";
export const assetUrl = (path: string): string => `${BASE_PATH}${path}`;
```

`config/` may not import from `engine/` (PLAN-3 §5), and this imports nothing — correct layer. The
e2e suite already runs with `NEXT_PUBLIC_BASE_PATH` set in CI, which makes it the natural regression
guard (§12.6).

### 10.3 What to acquire

| Bucket         | Count                                     | Notes                                            |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Ambience loops | 9 (one per `AmbienceId`)                  | 30–60 s, seamless, mono                          |
| Footsteps      | 4 surfaces × 3–4 variants                 | `carpet`, `hard`, `wet`, `gravel`                |
| Entity cues    | 4 (`growl`, `shriek`, `chitter`, `laugh`) | 1–2 s                                            |
| UI             | 2 (`click`, `pickup`)                     | optional — the synthesized ones are already good |

### 10.4 Licensing (non-negotiable)

- **CC0 / public domain only.** freesound.org filtered to CC0, opengameart.org CC0, Pixabay's sound
  library, archive.org public-domain material.
- **Reject CC-BY, CC-BY-SA and NonCommercial.** There is no credits surface anywhere in this app
  (verified: no "Credits" string exists in `src/`), so a CC-BY asset silently creates a license
  violation. If a CC-BY asset is genuinely worth it, the credits screen must be built _first_ and
  costed as part of the milestone.
- `public/audio/CREDITS.md` records, per file: source URL, author, license, date retrieved, and the
  original filename. README links to it. This file is the reason a future contributor can prove
  compliance without re-doing the search.

### 10.5 Format, size and encoding

- **`.ogg` (Vorbis), single format.** Shipping ogg + mp3 doubles repo bytes for a Safari
  version floor that the §10.1 fallback already covers gracefully. mp3's encoder padding also makes
  gapless ambience loops harder.
- Budget: **≤ 8 MB total added**; ambience loop ≤ 600 KB each, one-shot ≤ 30 KB each.
- Encode mono at a modest rate, e.g.
  `ffmpeg -i src.wav -ac 1 -ar 32000 -c:a libvorbis -q:a 1 out.ogg`.
- Layout: `public/audio/ambience/*.ogg`, `public/audio/sfx/footsteps/*.ogg`,
  `public/audio/sfx/entities/*.ogg`, `public/audio/CREDITS.md`.
- **Do not commit source `.wav`/`.flac` files.** Static export copies `public/` verbatim into `out/`,
  so anything committed there ships to every visitor.
- No `next.config.ts` change is needed; `.nojekyll` already exists.

## 11. 3D models — deferred, with the cost stated

**Recommendation: do not download 3D models in this phase.** Every prop in the game is currently a
merged set of unit boxes ([furnitureGeometry.ts](../src/components/scene/furnitureGeometry.ts)), which
is coherent with the low-fi liminal art direction, instances perfectly, costs zero bytes, needs no
license bookkeeping, and shares the one-material-set-per-level flyweight. A downloaded glTF prop
breaks the last of those (it brings its own materials/textures), which is exactly what the
play→menu→play leak test polices.

If the user still wants real models later, the checklist is:

- **`.glb` only**, ≤ 5k triangles, ≤ 512 px textures, **no DRACO/meshopt** (their decoders are extra
  files in `public/` and a version-coupling risk).
- CC0 only, same `CREDITS.md` discipline as audio; ≤ 2 MB each.
- Load via drei's `useGLTF` with `assetUrl()` (§10.2) and `useGLTF.preload` during the loading phase.
- **Dispose on unmount by traversal** — geometries, materials _and_ textures — or add them to a shared
  module-level cache that is deliberately never disposed. Pick one and comment which; the leak test
  will catch the third option (doing neither).
- Add a `<Suspense>` boundary; an un-suspended `useGLTF` throws inside the Canvas.

## 17. Design Notes

### D1 — Cosmetic-only structural features are the whole safety strategy

The world's two hard guarantees (border contract, connectivity) are proven over **cell values**,
while collision is resolved over **AABBs**. Anything that adds an obstacle without adding a cell value
can therefore invalidate a proof that no test is watching. So every feature this phase adds — door
frames, breaches, ceiling openings, pipe runs — is deliberately non-colliding and read-only, which
makes "did we break generation?" answerable by inspection rather than by luck. The one thing that
_does_ collide (ground props) is routed through the furniture pass, which already carries a corridor
fit test and a sub-cell flood-fill safety net. New content goes where the existing invariants already
live.

### D2 — Strategy table for surfaces, plain lookup for fixtures

`SURFACE_PAINTERS: Record<SurfaceStyle, SurfacePainter>` is a genuine Strategy: nine variants of the
same three operations, each with real behavior, and the set will grow whenever a level is added.
Replacing a `switch` there with a table keyed by the union also makes exhaustiveness a compile-time
property.

The fixture lookup in §7.4 is deliberately **not** the same thing: four entries of inert data
(geometry, offset, rotation) with no behavior. Making it a strategy object, a factory or a registry
would be pattern theater. Same file, same phase, different tools — because the two problems are
different shapes.

### D3 — Procedural textures stay; only audio is downloaded

The spec permits downloading assets. Audio is worth it: synthesized drones are convincing, but no
oscillator graph produces a real recording of dripping water in a concrete room, and the
`AudioEngine` interface was explicitly designed for this swap (PLAN.md §6). Surface textures are not:
canvas painters already parameterize on palette **and** decay, cost zero bytes, need no license file,
and match the deliberately low-fi art direction. Downloading nine sets of PBR maps would add
megabytes, licensing overhead and material-disposal risk to make the game look _less_ like itself.
Download where synthesis genuinely can't compete; synthesize everywhere else.

### D4 — Fallback-by-composition instead of feature detection

`SampledAudioEngine` holds a `fallback: AudioEngine` and delegates any cue it has no buffer for.
That single decision removes an entire class of work: no "are assets loaded yet" state in gameplay
code, no format capability detection, no blocking preload before entering play, no partial-manifest
special cases, and no silent-game failure mode. It is a Decorator over the interface the codebase
already depends on, and because the fallback is constructor-injected it is also the thing that makes
the class unit-testable with a spy. Both engines must share one `AudioContext`, and only one may
close it — the one place this composition needs care.

### D5 — Seeded entity RNG, taken because the file is already open

`updateWanderer` uses `Math.random()`, so entity behavior is not reproducible. That is tolerable for
one enemy with no behavior tests; it is not tolerable for three behavior strategies that need
assertions about chasing, freezing and damage rates. Threading an `Rng` through `spawn()` costs one
parameter and removes the need to mock a global in every behavior test. The world's determinism
contract is unaffected either way (entities are simulation, not generation) — this is purely a
testability seam, taken at the moment it is cheapest.

### D6 — Extend furniture, do not create a props system

Pipes, lockers and transformers need exactly what `placeFurniture` already provides: deterministic
seeded placement, per-level weights, a fit test, a walkable-corridor guarantee, a connectivity safety
net, exact colliders and instanced rendering. A parallel `engine/props/` would duplicate all of it to
express "this one is metal". The one real distinction — overhead objects need none of that machinery
and must not collide — is served by the feature system instead. Two systems, split on a property
that actually differs.

### D7 — Nine ambience ids, four synthesis recipes

Widening `AmbienceId` to nine gives each level its own audio-asset key without writing five new
oscillator graphs: `RECIPE_BY_AMBIENCE` maps the nine ids onto the four existing recipes. The data
model gains the resolution it needs; the synthesis code gains nothing to maintain. If a level later
deserves bespoke synthesis, it becomes a fifth recipe — an additive change to a lookup table.

### D8 — Splitting capability from authoring (M18–M23 vs M24)

Mixing "build the door system" with "decide how many doors Level 5 has" produces a diff where a
reviewer cannot separate a plumbing bug from a lore disagreement. Capability milestones ship with
provisional numbers and are reviewed against tests; M24 changes only data and is reviewed against the
wiki. This mirrors PLAN-3's insistence on keeping the pure-deletion diff separate from the
new-content diff, for the same reason.

### D9 — Naming the illusions out loud

Two things in this plan are deliberate fakes: the "hole" in the ceiling is a dark quad under an
uncut plane, and a yaw-jittered prop's collider is its axis-aligned expansion (inherited from M9).
Both are correct choices at this fidelity and both look like bugs to a reader who does not know why.
They get a one-line comment each — that is what comments are for. Nothing else in this phase should
be commented; `featureRates.doorway`, `flickerFactor` and `SURFACE_PAINTERS.bareRock` say what they
are.

### D10 — What this phase deliberately does not build

No hazard damage (steam, electricity, drowning), no falling, no openable doors, no per-fixture point
lights, no per-level entity reskins, no downloaded models or textures, no `humidity` field, no
`sconces` lighting mode, no floor holes, no credits screen (avoided by taking CC0 only), no new UI.
Each was considered and cut with a reason recorded above. The measure of this phase is whether nine
screenshots look like nine different places — not whether the engine grew.

## 18. Open questions for the user

1. **D0 — which wiki is canon?** Blocking. Recommendation: keep `backrooms.fandom.com` (the shipped
   roster). Choosing otherwise renames/rethemes levels 1, 4, 6 and 7 and needs an extra milestone.
2. **Level 0 entities.** If the wiki states Level 0 is entity-free, should it be — trading tension for

   real escalation.
3. **Passive entities on peaceful difficulty.** Today everything in `entityRegistry` is thinned by
   `enemyAggression`, so a harmless drifter also disappears on peaceful. Keep that simple invariant,
   or exempt non-damaging entities? Recommendation: keep it simple this phase.
4. **The Level 7 water plane** (§5.7) — build the cheap translucent plane, or ship `wetTile` + `wet`
   footsteps + drip audio only? Recommendation: the latter first; add the plane only if the level
   still reads as dry.
5. **Asset budget.** Is ~8 MB of committed CC0 audio acceptable in the repo and on Pages?
   Recommendation: yes, with per-level lazy fetching so no visitor downloads more than one loop.
